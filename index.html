    How to play
    
    
    
    Each hole is a five-letter word. Guess it in as few swings as you can —
      your guesses are your strokes.
    
    
      
      
    • Par 3 — a standard opener walks straight onto it.
      • 
      
    • Par 4 — honest golf. Most words live here.
      • 
      
    • Par 5 — the traps. Words like RIVER, where you can know
        four letters and still be guessing between five answers.
      • 
      
    • There is no failing a hole. Keep swinging until it drops and card
        whatever it cost — a seven is a seven.
      • 
      
    • The board opens at par, so the rows you can see are the strokes you
        are playing for. Go past it and it grows, in dashed red.
      • 
    
    
    
    Par is measured, not guessed: a solver plays every answer from standard
      openers, and par follows how many strokes it really costs.
    
    
    Your shot is how much is still unknown — how many answers could still be
      right. Cut the field from hundreds to a handful and you have striped one
      down the middle. Learn nothing and you have shanked it, wherever you were
      lying.
    
    
    Nine holes make a round. Same course code, same nine words, so you and
      your friends can play the exact same track.
    
    
    Answers are always ordinary words. You can guess anything in
      the dictionary — GONER, ZONER, RONEO are all accepted — but
      a hole's word is drawn from a much smaller list of everyday ones. When you
      are down to a handful of candidates, bet on the common one.
